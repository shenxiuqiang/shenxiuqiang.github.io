A friend of mine has worked in foreign trade for more than a decade. At dinner the other day he put it bluntly: over the next few years, if an ordinary young person wants to earn their first million, the opportunity he is most optimistic about is the new wave of Chinese exporters going global.

When most people hear “foreign trade,” they still picture setting up an Alibaba.com storefront, booking a booth at the Canton Fair, or sitting in an office waiting for overseas buyers to send an email. In reality, the way money is made in Chinese exporting has already changed several times over. Judge it with a ten-year-old mental model and you will miss the real opening.

## Five generations of the export business

**1.0 Selling capacity**

In the early years, Chinese factories made things and overseas buyers sold them. The buyer held the channel and the market, so the factory earned only a processing fee. Today they order from you, tomorrow the factory next door is two cents cheaper and the order is gone. This is a business of being chosen.

**2.0 Cross-border e-commerce**

Amazon, Shopee, TikTok Shop and similar platforms let sellers bypass traditional middlemen and reach consumers directly. But after a few years you notice the platform has become the new middleman — ad spend, commissions, price wars, return costs. None of them are cheap.

**3.0 Customer acquisition through overseas social media**

This is the stage where ordinary people can genuinely get in. You do not need to own a factory. You only need a relationship with one that can produce, and then film the production line, material comparisons, quality checks, and product details for TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Facebook. Wholesalers and retailers abroad see the content and message you on WhatsApp. You used to hunt the world for customers; now customers find you through content. And the small and mid-sized buyers who cannot afford to fly to the Canton Fair need Chinese supply chains just as much — they simply had no way to reach them before.

**4.0 AI-powered foreign trade**

Running overseas social media used to take a team: translators, video editors, channel managers, researchers, sales reps. Today one person who understands the business, working with a large language model and a set of AI workflows, can absorb most of what that small team did: research demand in a target country, produce multilingual content at volume, draft replies to inquiries, and read what a customer actually needs. A young person no longer has to own a factory to help a Chinese factory win overseas orders.

**5.0 Local operations abroad**

This is where the gap really opens up. Once social media has brought you a steady base of customers — say a shoe wholesaler in Morocco — and order volume picks up, you can start thinking about local warehousing, agents, showrooms, after-sales service and a distribution network. You move from earning a one-off trading margin to running a market for the long term. The logic flips too: instead of “whatever China makes, we sell,” it becomes “whatever foreigners need, we go back and organize the Chinese supply chain to make.”

## No money, no factory, no connections — how do you start?

His advice was plain: pick one country, one type of customer, one narrow product. Win customers through overseas social media, cut operating costs with AI, and deliver using China’s mature supply chain. Get the first order through the door, build up customers and cash flow, and only then think about overseas warehouses and localization. Do not set out to run a global business on day one.

One line from him stuck with me: China has never lacked people who can make things; what it lacks is people who can sell those things to the whole world. The owners of factories turning over tens of millions of yuan may be more desperate than you are to find a young person who understands overseas traffic. A walk through Yiwu, Foshan or Dongguan might bring you closer to the real opportunity than another résumé sent to a big tech company.

The essence of this new wave is not reinventing trade. It is using content and AI to reconnect the information gap between China’s supply chain and small and mid-sized buyers around the world. For ordinary people, it may be one of the few paths left that is not already crowded out — and that you can actually start with nothing but execution.
